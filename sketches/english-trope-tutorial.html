English Cantillation Tutorial

Map the Torah's musical grammar (te'amim) onto English sentences — hear the real Ashkenazi motifs on any words you type.

1 · Build a sentence

Click any word to cycle its cantillation mark (Mercha → Tipcha → Etnachta → Sof Pasuk). The notation below is the same note-line view used elsewhere in this app: each bar is a held pitch (in semitones from the tonic, 0 = home note), word boundaries are dashed, and the gold playhead tracks exactly what's playing — so what you see is always what you hear.

2 · The grammar of the music

These four marks are the load-bearing "punctuation" of a Torah verse. In the Ashkenazi tradition they don't just say how to sing — they tell you how the sentence is built. Press ▶ to hear each motif on its own (sung on a neutral "ah", the actual measured shape from recorded Torah readings).

3 · Practice the method

  1. Find the halfway point of your sentence and place an Etnachta there — the semicolon, where the melody drops to rest on the tonic.
  2. Find the comma (or natural breath) before that halfway point and mark it Tipcha — the comma, a gentle dip that leads into the rest.
  3. Mark everything else Mercha — the connectors that glue each word to the next, always leaning forward.
  4. The last word of the sentence always gets Sof Pasuk — the period, a full melodic cadence down to close the thought.

Type your own sentence above, click Auto-mark to see this method applied automatically, then click individual words to correct it by ear.

Melodic motifs, colors and note names are the real per-accent data from this project's Torah trope engine (js/trope.js, measured from recorded Ashkenazi Torah readings in js/audio.js) — reused here unmodified, just aimed at English words instead of Hebrew ones. Spoken words use your browser's built-in speech synthesis; the chant is synthesized live with the Web Audio API, no recordings involved. AI-assisted teaching tool — the trope-to-grammar mapping is a simplified teaching model, not a substitute for a Tikkun or a teacher.
